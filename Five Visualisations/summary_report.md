# Employee Data Analysis — Summary Report

## 1. Problem Statement
The objective of this project is to analyse employee salary data and identify differences across departments and cities using Python visualisations.

## 2. Dataset
The dataset contains **8 employees** with five fields: ID, name, department, salary, and city. The analysis uses Pandas for data analysis and Matplotlib for visualisation.

## 3. Key Insights

1. **Department-wise salary:** Finance has the highest average salary at ₹90,000, but it has only one employee. IT has the next-highest average salary at approximately ₹67,667, followed by HR at ₹46,500 and Sales at ₹41,000.

2. **Salary distribution:** Employee salaries range from ₹30,000 to ₹90,000, a difference of ₹60,000. The median salary is ₹56,000, showing the middle salary value in this dataset.

3. **City-wise salary:** Mumbai has the highest average salary at ₹69,000, followed by Bangalore at ₹56,000 and Chennai at ₹54,500. These differences may reflect the employees represented in this small dataset.

## 4. Recommendations

1. Review salary differences across departments and job responsibilities before making compensation decisions.
2. Analyse a larger employee dataset, including role, experience, and location, to identify more reliable salary patterns.

## 5. Limitations
The dataset contains only eight employees, and Finance is represented by just one employee. Therefore, these averages may not represent the organisation as a whole. The analysis describes patterns but does not establish their causes.

## Conclusion
The five visualisations help summarise employee salary patterns and compare departments and cities. A larger dataset would support more reliable business decisions.
